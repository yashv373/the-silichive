About

The Silichive is a personal technical archive — a place to document semiconductor knowledge, VLSI research, and engineering thinking in a structured, long-lasting format.

The name comes from Silicon + Archive. The idea is simple: build a living knowledge base that grows over time, capturing research notes, design insights, project documentation, and technical writing around chip design and computer architecture.

What this is

  • A digital research vault for semiconductor engineering
  • A personal notebook for VLSI, CMOS, and architecture concepts
  • A documentation space for ongoing projects and experiments
  • A curated collection of papers and technical references

What this is not

  • A portfolio website
  • A blog with opinions on trending topics
  • A startup landing page
  • A social media presence

Design philosophy

This site is built with plain HTML and CSS. No frameworks. No build tools. No databases. No JavaScript dependencies. It loads fast, reads clean, and stays out of the way. The terminal-inspired aesthetic is intentional — it reflects the environment where most of this work actually happens.

Clarity over complexity. Structure over decoration. Writing over visual noise. Longevity over trends.

The person behind this

I'm an engineering student working at the intersection of VLSI design, computer architecture, and semiconductor research. My interests span from transistor-level circuit design to system-level architecture exploration.

Areas I spend time on:

  • CMOS circuit design and physical design
  • Spintronics and emerging memory technologies
  • Hardware accelerator architecture
  • EDA tool development and synthesis optimization
  • Open-source chip design flows

Contact

The best way to reach me is through GitHub. This site is open source — you can see all the content in the repository.

Note

This archive is always growing. New notes, research entries, and project documentation are added regularly. If something is incomplete, it is probably being worked on.
